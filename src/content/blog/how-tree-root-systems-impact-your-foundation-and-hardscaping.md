Trees are a great addition to any property, offering shade, privacy, and curb appeal. What many homeowners do not realize is that the root system beneath the surface can sometimes create problems when trees are planted too close to structures. Over time, those roots can affect your foundation, driveway, and walkways in ways that are costly to repair.

## How Roots Interact with Structures

Tree roots grow outward in search of water and nutrients. As they expand, they often follow the path of least resistance. This means they are more likely to move into areas where soil is loose or where small cracks already exist. While roots are not strong enough to break solid concrete on their own, they can expand into tiny openings and gradually make them worse.

As roots thicken, they push against surrounding materials. This pressure can cause sidewalks to lift, driveways to shift, and existing cracks to widen. In areas like Northern New Jersey, where soil expands and contracts with seasonal temperature changes, the added movement from roots can speed up the damage.

## Signs of Root-Related Damage

Many issues caused by tree roots develop slowly, which makes them easy to overlook at first. You might notice sections of your sidewalk becoming uneven or parts of your driveway starting to rise. Cracks in concrete surfaces are another common warning sign.

Inside the home, early indicators can include small foundation cracks or doors and windows that no longer open and close as smoothly as they once did. These changes often point to the ground shifting, which may be influenced by nearby tree roots.

## Preventing Problems Before They Start

Prevention starts with proper planning. When planting new trees, it is important to think about how large the tree will grow and how far its roots may spread. Giving trees enough space away from your home and hardscaped areas can help avoid issues down the line.

For trees that are already established, routine maintenance is key. Professional trimming and inspections can help keep growth under control. In some cases, installing root barriers can guide roots away from vulnerable areas. If damage has already begun, a tree care professional can determine whether root pruning or removal is the best option.
