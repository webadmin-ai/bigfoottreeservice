When homeowners think about tree maintenance, they usually focus on what they can see above ground. Branches, leaves, and overall appearance often get the most attention. However, a tree’s root system can extend far beyond the trunk and sometimes create issues below the surface, especially when underground utilities are nearby.

Knowing how tree roots interact with utility infrastructure can help property owners avoid unexpected repairs and protect both their trees and their homes.

## Roots follow water

Tree roots are constantly searching for water and nutrients. If there is a small leak in a sewer line, water pipe, or drainage system, roots may naturally grow toward that moisture source. Contrary to popular belief, roots do not typically break through solid pipes. Instead, they take advantage of existing cracks, loose joints, or damaged sections.

Once roots find a source of water, they can continue expanding and create larger problems over time.

## Sewer Lines are a common target

One of the most frequent root-related issues involves sewer lines. Older pipes are especially vulnerable because they may have small openings that allow roots to enter. As roots grow inside the pipe, they can catch debris and gradually restrict the flow of wastewater.

Homeowners may begin noticing slow drains, recurring clogs, or unpleasant odors. If left untreated, root intrusion can lead to significant pipe damage and costly repairs.

## Drainage Problems Can Develop
Tree roots can also affect drainage systems. Storm drains, French drains, and other underground drainage solutions can become obstructed when roots grow into or around them. This may reduce the system’s ability to move water away from the property.

Poor drainage can result in standing water, soggy lawns, soil erosion, and even foundation concerns if excess moisture accumulates near the home.

## Water Lines and Other Utilities
Water lines can be impacted when roots encounter areas where moisture is escaping from the system. While healthy modern pipes are generally durable, older infrastructure may be more susceptible to root-related issues.

Roots can also complicate maintenance and repair work by growing around utility lines and making them more difficult to access.

## Prevention Is the Best Approach
The best way to reduce the risk of root-related utility problems is through proper planning or possible tree removal depending on how deep the roots are. Before planting a tree, consider its mature size and root spread. Large-growing species should be placed a safe distance away from underground utilities whenever possible.

Regular inspections can also help identify issues before they become serious. If you suspect tree roots may be affecting a utility line on your property, consulting a licensed tree care professional can help you determine the safest and most effective solution.
